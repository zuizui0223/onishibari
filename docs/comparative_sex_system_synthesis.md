# Daphne reproductive alternatives: a conditional hypothesis
Last revised: 2026-11-14 / Hypothesis, not proof.

## Target problem
Why can visually inconspicuous early-spring flowers maintain large, male-biased hermaphrodite flowers and morphologically persistent pistils, despite low pollinator visitation and potentially costly self-fertilization?

Rather than assuming that low visitation directly selects for selfing, distinguish **fertilization assurance** from **effective (viable) offspring assurance**. A plant can be physiologically self-compatible but gain almost no viable offspring through selfing if selfed embryos or seedlings fail.

## Three observed comparative states (species-specific evidence)
| Species | Verified result (original species only) | Alternative interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| *Daphne jezoensis* | Gynodioecious: hermaphroditic flowers are larger, males-biased with low seed fertility even after hand outcrossing; some selfed seeds form but have low germination and early inbreeding depression. 2017 and 2018 experimental results. | Persistent male-biased hermaphrodites can coexist with females despite very poor seed production. |
| *D. kiusiana* | Eastern lineage has smaller/fewer flowers, high selfing and elevated homozygosity relative to western lineage. Genetic demographic analysis infers a historical selfing syndrome (2023). | Small inconspicuous flowers may accompany **effective selfing**, not obligatory dependence on pollinators. |
| *D. laureola* | Across 38 Iberian populations, sex-system variation correlates with climatic conditions; warmer/drier regions include hermaphrodite-only populations, and flower tube lengths differ between female and hermaphroditic morphs. | Local ecology can affect sex-system maintenance; similar flower appearance does not imply equal fruiting. |
| **D. pseudomezereum** | Summer deciduous shrub with early spring green-yellow flowers and faint aroma (field guides/museum), with evidence of two floral morphs from field photographs, but direct experimental fitness and compatibility data currently unavailable. | **Main target:** test which functional state it actually occupies. |

## Competing mechanisms to be distinguished
1. **Self-pollen interference:** own pollen loads stigma and suppresses effective outcross pollen tubes; in male-biased morph, pollen production and female interference may jointly rise.
2. **Intrinsic loss of female function:** pollen tubes reach ovules yet female-derived seeds fail, including with full hand-outcross pollen doses.
3. **Early inbreeding depression:** own pollen fertilizes, but embryos/seedlings of selfed origin suffer failure.
4. **Low costs of pistil retention:** there is no selective benefit to complete loss of female organs when residual function is inexpensive.
5. **Reproductive assurance under rare colonization:** occasional selfed viable offspring could have selective benefit when no suitable mate is nearby; not assumed if inbreeding depression is severe.
6. **Pollinator niche and local sex ratio:** female success depends on nearby synchronized pollen-producing plants and effective day/night visitors, not just the overall population sex ratio.
7. **Independent seasonal resource allocation:** flowering during wintergreen spring light conditions may constrain flowering, reproduction and senescence; no causal link to sex has yet been confirmed.

## Heuristic decision rule (conceptual, not fitted evolutionary model)
The local *net viable seed value* of retaining selfing function is positive if:

**(additional selfed seeds that survive and reproduce) - (outcross offspring lost via self-pollen interference) - (female-function maintenance and fruiting costs) > 0.**

Potential selfed fruit count alone is inadequate: it must be weighted by germination and subsequent fitness. This inequality ignores male fitness and genetic transmission and is **not** a population-genetic stability criterion. It identifies what to measure; it does not predict sex frequencies.

## Discriminating plan (priority order)
1. **Phenotype inventory:** mark each *D. pseudomezereum* plant, measure crown and flower size separately, score whether anthers produce pollen, dissect pistils and ovules. Use photographic vouchers, genotype-check uncertain taxa. Estimate local flower-producing plant density and distance.
2. **Cross-compatibility design:** female and hermaphroditic morphs receiving identical outcross pollen, alongside selfed and unpollinated treatments. Measure fruit, seed, germination and offspring survival.
3. **Pollen competition:** emasculated hermaphroditic flowers get X+X2 vs X+S mixed pollen with equal total grains; track known pollen donors, pollen tubes and embryos; verify that results do not reflect emasculation injury.
4. **Day/night and early seasonal pollination:** visible visits, stigma contact, pollen deposition, bagged day vs night, scent GC-MS if available.
5. **Selective interpretation:** contrast viable offspring and pollen paternity, not fruit percentage alone; if pollen paternity cannot be measured, restrict conclusions to female/seed function.
6. **Macro context:** GBIF 649 Japanese *D. pseudomezereum* occurrences (362 georeferenced) versus just 26 recent geo/photo records. Focus macro on distribution/environmental context, not a sex-ratio inference. iNaturalist research-grade photos 28 events; 14 open-flower primary images, 4 fruit images, **zero functionally sexed from photos**.

## What falsifies the motivating claim?
- If hermaphrodites outcross as successfully as females, the hypothesized intrinsic female-function loss is not supported.
- If X+S and X+X2 have equal outcross pollen tube success after balancing donor quality and dose, self-pollen obstruction is not supported in that setting.
- If viable selfed offspring are common and robust, functional near-dioecy is not necessarily the right characterization.
- If flower size does not increase pollen export after controlling for plant size and flower number, a floral attraction/male allocation explanation is weakened.
- If females do not respond to pollen supplementation, pollen limitation may not be the dominant local cause of low fruit set.

## Causal inference warning
Published results for related species support comparative *motivation*, not the mechanism in the target. Photographs do not establish self-incompatibility, odor chemistry, pollinator scarcity or a species-level sex ratio.
