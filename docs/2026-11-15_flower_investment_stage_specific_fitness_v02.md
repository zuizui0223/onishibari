# Stage-specific conversion of floral investment to reproductive success (evidence audit v0.2)
Date: 2026-11-15. Branch: research/angiosperm-reproductive-decoupling-20261115. Status: primary-paper audit + audited earlier-repo estimates; **no new plant-level reproductive dataset or causal fit**.

## A more falsifiable question
At what transition does additional flower number or *true floral construction/maintenance biomass* cease to increase **viable maternal offspring** or **genetically assigned paternal offspring**, and under which pollinator, resource, sex-morph, phenological and phylogenetic conditions does the limiting transition switch?

"More flowers" and "more floral biomass" are different exposures. Individual vegetative dry mass, canopy AGB and satellite NDVI are not floral investment. For flowers, record count, floral-unit definition, mean flower dry mass, flower-hours, nectar/scent/maintenance costs, seed/fruit investment and whole-plant reserves separately.

## Stage accounting / identification
Female success: potential flowers -> reproductive flowers exposed -> compatible-pollen deposition -> fertilized ovules -> mature viable seeds -> established offspring. Male success: floral display -> effective pollen export -> outcross/same-population/distant paternity -> surviving sired offspring. Count nonvisitation separately from ineffective visits, and distinguish flower-level fruit-set probability from ovule-level completion (different denominators). Adult survival and subsequent seasons are required for lifetime success.

Use the plant, not the flower, as the independent allocation unit; genetic individual rather than ramet for clonal plants. For each transition, retain the numerator and denominator as counts rather than calculating a disconnected set of per-flower ratios. Flowers taken destructively for pollen tubes/embryos are **different cohorts** from flowers followed to mature fruit; never multiply observed conditional proportions from disjoint cohorts as if they were sequential observations of the same flowers.

## New quantitative interpretation: vegetative biomass does not imply proportional fruit set
In the 2026 Brassica experiment, the *adjusted association* between ln vegetative mass and plant fruit-set log odds was beta=-0.321 with SE=0.148 (model also includes pollination treatment and raceme). Expressed per doubling of vegetative mass, OR = exp(beta*ln 2) = 0.8005, approximate Wald 95% CI 0.6547 to 0.9788. This is a **fractional fruit-set effect**; total fruits may still increase with biomass. It is not a causal mass manipulation and not specifically floral construction biomass. This is a useful counterexample to the assumption that every extra flower or gram of vegetative matter produces proportionate fruit output, but not proof of wasted allocation.

## Mechanistic regime switches: predictions, not results
1. **Encounter regime**: no effective visitor events. More floral exposure may lower chance of a zero-visit period even when visits per unit fall. Estimate first effective arrival from actual open-flower-hours and effort, not from species presence.
2. **Compatibility/transfer regime**: landing occurs, but compatible conspecific pollen or functioning pollen tubes are absent. Control pollen-donor identity and distinguish stigma load, compatible grains, tubes and genetically confirmed fertilization.
3. **Fertilization/ovule regime**: compatible grains present yet receptive/viable ovules are not fertilized. Direct sampled-ovule assay and sham crosses needed.
4. **Development regime**: fertilization succeeds but fruit/seed maturation is restricted by allocation, hormones, embryo quality or seed predation. Cross pollen addition with a resource/fruit-load manipulation and monitor later flowers.
5. **Paternal export regime**: more flowers increase plant visits but cause geitonogamy or within-genet recirculation. Track actual sired offspring and pollen export; visit counts cannot substitute for paternal fitness.
6. **Recruitment/temporal regime**: mature seeds do not recruit, or repeated failed years occur. Track germination, progeny survival, adult persistence and interannual autocorrelation; longevity is not automatic compensation.

A schematic female output capacity bound is S <= min(F * p_poll * o * q_fert * q_viable, K_seed); this is **not an estimated model** and its simplified factors need not be independent. Harder & Hamnett-Mount (2026) provide a more explicit simultaneous flower/fruit/seed-resource model (their Eq. 1), including the cost per fruit and per seed. Do not claim this principle as novel. The added cross-clade question is which transitions control **male and female** fitness under different environmental *reliability*, phylogenetic starting conditions and floral investment costs.

## Pre-registered tests that can actually locate a break
**T1. Exposure vs attractiveness**: with repeated flower-level phenology and true floral dry mass, randomize visual display down/up using sham and unchanged genuine flowers where possible; contrast against carefully controlled flower removal (changes physiology/resources) to separate advertising from carbon expenditure. Response: probability of first **effective** pollen event and its hazard.

**T2. Pollen vs allocation**: within verified sex morphs, factorial pollen availability (open, sham, compatible outcross addition; bagged self and bagged cross as separate fertility diagnostics) and resource capacity / fruit-load manipulation when ethically permitted. Outcome: fruiting from tagged flowers across chronological position, fertilized ovules, embryo abortion, mature seed and viability. A pollen-addition effect does **not** exclude simultaneous resource limitation.

**T3. Male vs female marginal returns**: genetic paternity of offspring versus viable maternal seeds per original genet for the same manipulated floral display; model increasing total and decreasing per-flower returns separately. For species with an effectively male-type and a female-type morph, do not combine them into one reproductive response.

**T4. Reliability and legacy**: replicate the same population over multiple springs, include flower and partner phenologies, consecutive no-effective-visit runs, partner density, sex morph, relatedness/ancestral state. Do not infer a lineage transition from cross-species trait association or cpDNA/nrITS discordance alone.

## Minimal data schema (not a claim of complete data)
Individual genet x population x year x date x flower ID: plant height/estimated vegetative biomass; true dry mass/flower and total floral mass; sex morph validated; total produced flowers and flower-days; zero or effective visitor arrival; conspecific/compatible pollen; pollen tube (destructive replicate); fertilized ovules (destructive replicate); fruit setting; mature seeds; seed viability; seedling recruitment; mother and inferred father of sampled progeny; pollinator observation effort and detection; weather, canopy, floral neighbors, compatible-donor abundance; experimental assignment and execution flags.

Priority for onishibari: first verify functional sex morph by bagged self/cross and other-morph positive controls, then measure flower number, flower dry mass and stage-specific failures **at the same genets**. The existing 2027 staged randomized pilot and conservation cap on flower manipulation are retained; don't enlarge manipulations simply to fill all missing fields. No private site or plant coordinates enter this public branch.

## Claims ruled out by current data
- UK PoMS "0.169" is **not** the causal coefficient for adding flowers to a Daphne plant.
- A weak ovule-fill correlation is **not** a demonstrated failure of fertilization, nor proof of resource limitation.
- The Brassica fruit-set mass coefficient is **not** the effect of an extra gram of floral biomass.
- Different taxon studies cannot be chained into a single numerical pollination-to-fitness conversion product.
- Presence of pollen on a stigma is **not** proof of compatible fertilization; successful fruit set is **not** proof of viable offspring.
- Evolutionary advantage is a marginal effect on total male + female fitness, accounting for future years, not a high per-flower ratio alone.
