# Ecological/morphological cross-check for cytonuclear discordance (2026-11-15)

## The conditional interpretation has changed
Han et al. (2025), Table 1 and associated descriptions, explicitly report several trait contrasts favoring Korean C + Kamchatkan K over Japanese P:

| Character | Korea C | Japan P | Kamchatka K |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rhizomes | developed, some ramets | absent | developed, some ramets |
| Anthers near upper calyx | fully enclosed | partly exserted | fully enclosed |
| Calyx lobes | white | pale green | white |
| Floral-season leaf arrangement | palm-like distal cluster | distributed along the stem | palm-like distal cluster |
| Calyx-tube length (mm, published range) | 3.3–5.9 | 5–9 | 3.5–6 |
| Calyx-lobe length (mm, published range) | 1.7–3.4 | 2.5–5.5 | 2.0–3.5 |
| Calyx tube color | dark red-purple | pale green | white |

Published range *midpoints* are C 4.6 / K 4.75 / P 7.0 mm for tube, and C 2.55 / K 2.75 / P 4.0 mm for lobe. **These are not population sample means and must not be used for statistical tests of divergence**.

This phenotype concordance **agrees with the chloroplast C–K tree** and contrasts with nrITS P–C. This is relevant to the conditional causal hierarchy:

- **IF** independently sequenced low-copy nuclear loci support C–K, then C–K is favored as the species-tree hypothesis, and the marker requiring explanation is the **P-like C nrITS**. Candidates include (i) historical rDNA nuclear introgression plus concerted evolution, (ii) ancestral ITS lineage polymorphism / ILS, (iii) ITS paralogy or PCR copy bias, (iv) unsampled ghost donors. Chloroplast capture from K into C would not be the simplest account, but cannot be ruled out.
- **IF** the nuclear species tree supports P–C, then K-like chloroplast in C is consistent with plastid capture or plastid ILS. Shared C–K traits may be ecological convergence, ancestral character retention, or linked life-history evolution.
- **IF** independent nuclear gene trees show discordance symmetric around the MSC-predicted alternatives, ILS becomes consistent; if highly asymmetric, assess gene flow, ancestral structure, gene-tree error and ghost lineages.

## Why morphology is not decisive
- A suite of characters can be developmentally/ecologically correlated, so counting 6 matching traits is not equivalent to 6 independent loci.
- Floral measurements were compiled from separate sources; no joint common-garden comparison or sex-morph standardization.
- Current taxonomic designation **var. koreana** is infraspecific. The 2025 paper documents that warm-temperate Honshu/Shikoku montane plants historically named var. koreana probably represent other entities; avoid folding them into Korea C.
- Sex systems differ across nearby Daphne: P reported morphologically dioecious; K described as hermaphroditic by Nitta & Uchida (2020); C functional sex is not yet experimentally established. **Do not infer reproductive isolation or speciation from morphology alone.**
- Under angiosperm chloroplast maternal inheritance, a plastid introgression would often imply a maternal donor, but maternal inheritance must not be assumed specifically in this complex without verification.

## Most decisive next study
Paired cp + independent nuclear targets on the **same vouchers** from C Korea, K (Kamchatka/Hokkaido), P (Japan), plus Japanese montane unresolved OTU; measure sexual morph/function per individual and ploidy. Test MSC tree vs introgression network; then connect sexual dimorphism/seasonality to the resolved history. Genus-level 2026 Angiosperms353 hybrid-speciation study (doi:10.1093/aob/mcag358) shows target enrichment is feasible but does not resolve the East Asian lineage.
